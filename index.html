Theory & Mechanics

Core Aerodynamic Physics

How wing geometry generates lift through pressure differentials and momentum conservation.

🌊

1. Bernoulli's Principle & Flow Velocity

As fluid flows over a curved upper wing surface (camber), the streamline cross-sectional area constricts, accelerating the local fluid velocity.

P + ½ ρ v² = \text{Constant}

Higher velocity results in a local decrease in static pressure on the top surface, creating a net upward suction force relative to the higher static pressure underneath.

⚡

2. Newton's Third Law & Downwash

Airfoils impart a downward momentum vector to the air stream passing over and beneath the wing surface.

F_{\text{lift}} = \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t} = \dot{m} \cdot v_{\text{downwash}}

For every action pushing mass downwards, an equal and opposite force reacts upward on the wing structure, directly generating physical lift.

📐

3. The Fundamental Lift Equation

Aerodynamic lift scales quadratically with airspeed velocity and linearly with dynamic air density and wing surface area.

L = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \rho \cdot v^2 \cdot S \cdot C_L

Where $C_L$ represents the dimensionless lift coefficient determined by airfoil shape and angle of attack (α).

🌪️

4. Boundary Layer Separation & Stall

When the Angle of Attack (α) exceeds the critical limit (~15° for standard airfoils), the adverse pressure gradient becomes too steep for the boundary layer to remain attached.

\alpha > \alpha_{\text{crit}} \implies \text{Airflow Separation}

The flow separates into turbulent eddies, causing $C_L$ to plummet and drag to spike dramatically.
