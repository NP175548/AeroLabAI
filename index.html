Variable Reference Guide

A detailed breakdown of all physical variables used in the lift and drag equations.

Air Density (ρ)

The mass of air per unit volume. Higher air density at lower altitudes generates greater dynamic pressure and lift.

Standard Unit: kg/m³

Airspeed (v)

The velocity of oncoming air relative to the wing. Lift increases with the square of speed ($v^2$).

Standard Unit: m/s (or km/h)

Reference Wing Area (S)

The total surface area of the wing projected onto a flat plane. Larger surface areas capture more fluid momentum.

Standard Unit: m²

Lift Coefficient (CL)

A dimensionless coefficient that expresses the ratio of lift pressure to dynamic pressure, dependent on wing shape and Angle of Attack.

Dimensionless

Drag Coefficient (CD)

A dimensionless coefficient quantifying the air resistance of an object, combining parasitic skin friction and induced drag.

Dimensionless

Angle of Attack (α)

The angle between the wing's chord line and the oncoming relative airflow. Increasing α increases lift up to the stall point.

Standard Unit: Degrees (°)

Dynamic Pressure (q)

The kinetic energy per unit volume of the moving fluid stream, calculated as $q = \frac{1}{2} \rho v^2$.

Standard Unit: Pascals (Pa)

Lift-to-Drag Ratio (L/D)

A measure of aerodynamic efficiency. Higher values indicate greater distance traveled per unit of energy lost to drag.

Dimensionless Ratio

Aerodynamic Principles

Understanding fluid dynamics and wing physics.

1. Bernoulli's Principle

As air accelerates over the curved upper surface of an airfoil, dynamic pressure rises and static pressure drops, producing an upward pressure differential force.

2. Newton's Third Law

The angled wing deflects oncoming air mass downwards (downwash). An equal and opposite upward reaction force is exerted on the airframe.
