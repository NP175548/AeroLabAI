Aerodynamic Variable Key

Comprehensive legend defining physical symbols, standard international units, and flight mechanics roles.

L

Lift Force

Unit: Kilonewtons (kN) or Newtons (N)

Perpendicular force generated relative to oncoming airflow, supporting aircraft weight against gravity.

D

Drag Force

Unit: Kilonewtons (kN) or Newtons (N)

Parallel retarding force opposing forward motion, composed of parasite skin friction and induced drag.

α

Angle of Attack (AoA)

Unit: Degrees (°)

Angle between the airfoil chord line and oncoming relative wind. Positive pitch increases lift up to critical stall.

v

True Airspeed

Unit: Meters per second (m/s)

Velocity of aircraft relative to surrounding air. Dynamic pressure increases quadratically with speed.

S

Planform Wing Area

Unit: Square meters (m²)

Total surface projected area of wing platform. Larger surface area generates greater total force.

ρ

Air Density

Unit: Kilograms per cubic meter (kg/m³)

Mass density of fluid medium. Decreases with increasing altitude and temperature (Sea level standard = 1.225 kg/m³).

m

Aircraft Mass

Unit: Kilograms (kg)

Total mass of airframe, fuel, and payload determining weight force required to sustain level flight.

CL / CD

Force Coefficients

Unit: Dimensionless

Dimensionless aerodynamic shape indices representing efficiency based on camber, angle of attack, and aspect ratio.

Mathematical Framework & Physics Formulas

Governing equations of incompressible fluid mechanics and flight dynamic stability.

1. Fundamental Aerodynamic Lift Equation

Total lift force generated by pressure differential across upper and lower airfoil surfaces:

L = ½ · ρ · v² · S · CL
  • L: Lift Force (Newtons)
  • ρ: Fluid Density (kg/m³)
  • v: Flow Velocity (m/s)
  • S: Wing Area (m²)
  • CL: Lift Coefficient

2. Total Drag & Induced Resistance Model

Sum of parasitic drag friction and vortex-induced drag from lift generation:

D = ½ · ρ · v² · S · [ CD0 + (CL² / (π · e · AR)) ]
  • CD0: Parasite Drag Coefficient (≈ 0.02)
  • AR: Aspect Ratio (Wing Aspect)
  • e: Oswald Efficiency Factor (≈ 0.85)

3. Bernoulli's Incompressible Flow Principle

Conservation of energy along streamlines establishing pressure gradients:

Pstatic + ½ · ρ · v² = Ptotal (Constant)

Faster air stream over curved upper camber reduces local static pressure, generating net upward force.

4. Flow Separation & Stall Dynamics

Boundary layer detachment when exceeding critical angle of attack (αcrit ≈ 15°):

α > αcrit ⇒ Rapid CL Drop + High Turbulent Drag

Airflow detaches from upper wing surface, producing a recirculating turbulent wake and loss of lift.
