Fundamental Aerodynamic Principles

How physics, pressure gradients, and air momentum combine to generate flight.

1. Bernoulli's Principle

As fluid velocity increases, its static pressure decreases simultaneously. An airfoil's curved upper surface forces air to travel faster over the top, creating a lower pressure zone above the wing relative to beneath it.

$$p + \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 + \rho g h = \text{Constant}$$

2. Newton's Third Law

For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. As the wing moves through air with a positive Angle of Attack ($\alpha$), it physically deflects oncoming air downward (downwash). The reaction pushes the wing upward.

$$\vec{F}_{\text{lift}} = -\frac{d\vec{p}_{\text{air}}}{dt}$$

3. Angle of Attack & Stall Physics

Increasing Angle of Attack increases lift up to a critical threshold ($\alpha_{\text{crit}} \approx 15^\circ$). Beyond this point, smooth laminar airflow can no longer follow the top wing contour, separating into turbulent eddies and causing sudden loss of lift.

$$C_{D,\text{induced}} = \frac{C_L^2}{\pi \cdot AR \cdot e}$$

4. Wing Design Trade-Offs

High Aspect Ratio wings (like gliders) minimize induced drag, ideal for low-speed endurance. Low Aspect Ratio delta wings (like fighter jets) offer structural strength and high agility at supersonic speeds at the cost of higher induced drag during turns.

$$\text{Aspect Ratio } (AR) = \frac{b^2}{S}$$
