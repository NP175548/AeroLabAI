Aerodynamic Foundations

Core fluid dynamics principles explaining physical mechanisms behind wing lift production.

🌪️

Bernoulli's Principle

As fluid flow speed increases, static pressure decreases. An asymmetric airfoil forces air moving over the curved upper surface to travel faster than air underneath, producing a pressure differential resulting in upward suction lift.

⚖️

Newton's Third Law

For every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction. As the angled underside of an airfoil redirects oncoming airflow downward (downwash), the air exerts an equal force directing the wing upward.

📐

Angle of Attack (α)

The angle between the airfoil's chord line and the relative wind vector. Increasing the angle increases downward deflection and airflow acceleration, elevating lift up to the critical stall limit.

⚠️

Boundary Layer Separation (Stall)

Exceeding the critical angle of attack forces air stream flow to detach from the upper wing surface, creating turbulent eddies, a severe drop in lift coefficient, and a sharp spike in form drag.
