Aerodynamic Fundamentals

How Airplane Wings Work

01

Bernoulli's Principle

As air flows over an asymmetrical cambered airfoil, the airflow path over the curved top surface is constricted, causing it to accelerate. According to Bernoulli's conservation of energy principle ($P + \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 = \text{const}$), faster fluid speed produces lower static pressure on top of the wing relative to the bottom.

$$P_{\text{top}} < P_{\text{bottom}} \implies F_{\text{net}} \text{ (Upward Lift)}$$
02

Newton's Third Law

A wing acts as an inclined deflector plane. As the lower and upper surfaces direct the passing mass of air downward (downwash), an equal and opposite reaction force pushes the wing upward and backward.

$$F_{\text{lift}} = \frac{\Delta p_{\text{downward}}}{\Delta t}$$
03

Angle of Attack & Stall

Increasing the Angle of Attack ($\alpha$) increases pressure differential and lift linearly up to a critical point. Beyond $\approx 15^\circ$, the smooth boundary layer detaches, creating turbulent eddies, loss of lift, and massive drag increase.

$$\alpha > \alpha_{\text{crit}} \implies \text{Boundary Layer Separation}$$
04

The Lift Equation

Total lift force is calculated through dynamic pressure, wing area, and dimensionless lift coefficient ($C_L$), which accounts for airfoil shape and angle of attack.

$$L = \frac{1}{2} \, \rho \, V^2 \, S \, C_L$$
