Aerodynamic Principles & Physics Equations

The mathematical and fluid mechanics foundations governing heavier-than-air flight.

1. Fundamental Lift Equation

Aerodynamic lift is produced by the dynamic pressure of air moving around the wing surface:

$$L = \frac{1}{2} \rho v^2 S C_L$$
  • $\rho$ (Rho): Air Density ($\text{kg/m}^3$)
  • $v$: Relative Wind Velocity ($\text{m/s}$)
  • $S$: Wing Reference Area ($\text{m}^2$)
  • $C_L$: Coefficient of Lift (Dimensionless)

2. Total Drag & Induced Drag

Total aerodynamic drag consists of parasitic zero-lift drag combined with lift-induced drag:

$$D = \frac{1}{2} \rho v^2 S \left( C_{D0} + \frac{C_L^2}{\pi \cdot e \cdot AR} \right)$$
  • $C_{D0}$: Zero-Lift Parasite Drag Coefficient
  • $AR$: Wing Aspect Ratio ($\frac{b^2}{S}$)
  • $e$: Oswald Efficiency Factor ($\approx 0.8 - 0.85$)

3. Bernoulli's Principle & Dynamic Pressure

Conservation of energy in incompressible fluid flow states that an increase in speed causes a simultaneous decrease in static pressure:

$$P_{\text{static}} + \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 = P_{\text{total}} = \text{Constant}$$

Faster moving airflow over the curved upper chamber creates lower static pressure, resulting in upward pressure differential forces.

4. Stall Physics & Critical Angle

When angle of attack exceeds critical threshold ($\alpha_{\text{crit}} \approx 15^\circ$), adverse pressure gradient forces boundary layer airflow separation:

$$C_L(\alpha > \alpha_{\text{crit}}) \propto \sin(2\alpha) \cdot e^{-\lambda (\alpha - \alpha_{\text{crit}})}$$

The turbulent wake behind the wing rapidly decreases lift generation while dramatically increasing pressure drag.
