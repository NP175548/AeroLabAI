Aerodynamic Principles & Equations

Mathematical framework governing fluid mechanics and aerodynamic lift generation.

1. Fundamental Lift Formula

Aerodynamic lift is proportional to dynamic fluid pressure, wing area, and camber angle coefficient:

L = ½ · ρ · v² · S · CL
  • L: Lift Force (Newtons)
  • ρ: Air Density (kg/m³)
  • v: Flow Velocity (m/s)
  • S: Planform Wing Area (m²)
  • CL: Lift Coefficient

2. Total Drag & Induced Resistance

Drag combines zero-lift parasite friction with lift-dependent wingtip induced drag:

D = ½ · ρ · v² · S · [ CD0 + (CL² / (π · e · AR)) ]
  • CD0: Parasite Drag Coefficient
  • AR: Aspect Ratio (b² / S)
  • e: Oswald Efficiency Factor (≈ 0.8)

3. Bernoulli's Pressure Differential

Flow acceleration over the upper curved surface causes a localized drop in static pressure:

Pstatic + ½ · ρ · v² = Ptotal (Constant)

Higher velocity over the top camber reduces local static pressure relative to the flat bottom, creating a net upward force.

4. Boundary Layer Stall Mechanics

Exceeding the critical angle (αcrit ≈ 15°) induces boundary layer separation:

α > αcrit ⇒ Rapid Lift Loss & High Turbulence

Airflow detaches from the upper surface, creating a turbulent recirculating wake that sharply cuts lift while increasing pressure drag.
